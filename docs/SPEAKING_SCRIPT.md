# Speaking Script — The Honest Price Tag (Team Ctrl+V)

**Format:** ~10-11 min talk + Q&A, two presenters.
**Split:** **A** owns the story, the product, and the live demo. **B** owns the scoring brain, the data
insights, and feasibility. Both field questions.
**Golden rules:** do not read the slides, the slide is the backdrop. Look up. Pause after the numbers.
Have a recorded demo video ready in a tab in case the live site stalls. Assign A and B to your two names.

Timing per slide is a guide. The demo (slide 11) is the heart, give it room.

---

## Slide 1 — Title (A) ~45s
> "Hi everyone, we are team Ctrl+V. I want to open with a real product from the catalog you gave us.
> This phone speaker carries a Cradle to Cradle eco certificate. There is a green badge on the box.
> But look at the actual data underneath it. It is wrapped in plastic. It is flown in from Japan. And it
> takes a hundred and sixty eight kilos of CO2 just to manufacture. So is it really a good, sustainable
> choice? Our system says no. It gives it two leaves out of five, and it refuses to put a trust badge on
> it. That gap, between what a product claims and what it actually is, is the whole problem we set out to
> solve. We call our project The Honest Price Tag."

*Cue: point at the speaker card on the slide as you list plastic, Japan, 168 kg. Let the number land before "no".*

---

## Slide 2 — The problem (A) ~40s
> "Here is the thing we all recognise. People genuinely want to shop greener. Survey after survey says so.
> But the moment they are actually in the shop, the sustainability information is vague, it is unverifiable
> jargon, or it is just not there. So a shopper who cares ends up doing the easy thing anyway: the cheapest
> item, with the fastest delivery. The good intention is there. The information they would need to act on it
> is not. We wanted to close that gap inside the ten seconds a real person actually has."

*Cue: "ten seconds" slowly. Transition to slide 3.*

---

## Slide 3 — Bol's promise (A) ~40s
> "And we did not want to invent a mission for Bol, because Bol already has one. Bol calls itself the winkel
> van ons allemaal, the shop that belongs to all of us. And Bol explicitly says it wants three things to go
> together: gemak, betaalbaarheid en duurzaamheid. Convenience, affordability, and sustainability. The
> problem today is that shoppers feel they have to choose between them. Our entire layer is built to put all
> three on the same product. Keep that trio in mind, because we are going to prove with the data that they
> really can coexist."

*Cue: this is the emotional anchor. Slow, warm. Hand to B is on slide 5, so you keep 4 too.*

---

## Slide 4 — The solution (A) ~40s
> "So what is it. It is a thin sustainability layer that sits inside the normal Bol shopping flow. Four parts.
> One, a single score shown as leaves, right next to the price, so you get it at a glance. Two, a reason you
> can actually read, in plain language, so it is never a black box. Three, a greener alternative from the same
> category, that is usually also cheaper. And four, a quiet check on the seller, so we notice when a label does
> not add up. The important word is thin. It sits on top of shopping. It never takes the experience over.
> Now I will hand over to [B] to open up the brain behind the score."

*Handoff to B.*

---

## Slide 5 — How the score works (B) ~50s
> "Thanks. So the headline is one number, but underneath it we score four stages of a product's life. We call
> them Make, Move, Pack, and Last. Make is the manufacturing footprint, the CO2 to produce it. Move is how far
> it travels from its country of origin to the Netherlands. Pack is the packaging, plus which Bol warehouse it
> ships from, because that matters, and I will come back to it. And Last is durability, repairability and
> lifespan. Each of these is ranked against other products in the same category, so a treadmill is never
> judged against a digital planner, and then blended into one number from zero to a hundred. The weights are
> not arbitrary. They follow the EU's Product Environmental Footprint method, where climate change carries the
> most weight of any single category, which is why Make is the largest at thirty five percent."

*Cue: tap each of the four boxes as you name them. "Not arbitrary" answers the obvious judge question pre-emptively.*

---

## Slide 6 — Insight: making, not shipping (B) ~45s
> "Now, a careful judge will immediately ask: if Make is CO2 and Move is also about transport, are you not
> counting carbon twice. We asked ourselves the same thing, so we tested it. Across the entire catalog, the
> CO2 figure barely moves with how far a product ships. The correlation is zero point one two, which is
> basically nothing. And every digital product reads zero CO2. That is the fingerprint of a manufacturing
> footprint, not a transport one. So Make and Move are genuinely measuring different things, and no gram of
> carbon is double counted. This is the kind of check we ran before trusting any field in the data."

*Cue: point to the big 0.12. This slide buys you enormous credibility with the engineers. Say "we tested it" with confidence.*

---

## Slide 7 — Insight: honest about gaps (B) ~45s
> "The second hard truth is that the data is full of holes, on purpose. Fifty two percent of recyclability
> values are blank. Forty nine percent of lifespans. Twenty six percent of carbon footprints. A lot of teams
> would quietly paper over that. We do the opposite. Every score carries a confidence level, set by how
> complete its underlying data is, and low confidence scores visibly fade and say so. And here is the neat
> part. That same completeness number is effectively a readiness score for the EU Digital Product Passport,
> which is going to require these exact fields very soon. So admitting what we do not know is not a weakness
> here, it is a head start."

*Cue: "on purpose" with a small smile, it shows you understood the brief's intent.*

---

## Slide 8 — The check (B) ~45s
> "This is also where we catch the fibs. A small, deterministic rule engine compares what a seller claims
> against what the data shows. So for example: a carbon neutral badge on an item that sits in the dirtiest
> quarter of its category for CO2. Or an eco label on something plastic, far shipped, and high footprint. Or
> a recyclable claim on material that is barely recyclable. When the claim and the data disagree, we simply
> withhold the trust badge. And notice the stance here, because the brief asked about this directly. We inform
> the shopper, we do not shame the seller. There is no red warning on the storefront. The product just does
> not earn the badge, and the reasons are there if you look."

*Cue: this answers "are you throwing third party sellers under the bus". Land "inform, not shame" clearly.*

---

## Slide 9 — Insight: greener is not pricier (B) ~40s
> "And now the myth I most wanted to bust, and it ties straight back to Bol's promise. We checked whether
> being greener means paying more. In Bol's catalog, price and sustainability are essentially unrelated. The
> correlation is zero point zero three. And for eighty one percent of products, there is a greener option in
> the same category that also costs less. That is why our nudge leads with the saving, not with guilt.
> Convenience, affordability, and sustainability really can sit on the same product. We are not asking
> shoppers to sacrifice anything. Back to [A] to show what that feels like at checkout."

*Cue: point to 0.03 then 81%. Callback to slide 3's trio. Handoff to A.*

---

## Slide 10 — Make the impact real (A) ~40s
> "Thanks. So per product, the saving from one greener swap is honestly tiny. A few cents, a kilo of CO2. Not
> motivating on its own. But across a whole basket, it adds up fast. So at checkout we total the money saved
> and the CO2 saved, and then we translate that carbon into something a person can actually picture. Not a
> number of kilograms, which means nothing to most of us, but a trip. A drive to Rotterdam. A drive to Berlin.
> A short flight to London. And those are real equivalents, a car at a hundred and seventy grams per kilometre,
> flights from standard per passenger figures. Let me just show you all of this live."

*Cue: this sets up the demo. Move to the browser now.*

---

## Slide 11 — LIVE DEMO (A drives, B on standby for tech questions) ~3 min
*Switch to the live site. A narrates and clicks. Keep it calm, do not rush the clicks.*

1. **Storefront.** > "This is a Bol style shop. Same browsing you know. The only new thing is here on every
   card: a small leaf score and, on some, a blue Goede Keuze badge. Nothing has taken over the page."
2. **Sort.** > "I can sort by sustainability, high to low, and the greenest products rise to the top. Notice
   digital goods are kept out of this ranking, so a software subscription does not beat a bamboo toothbrush."
3. **Open a strong product.** > "Let me open one. Here is the score as five leaves, with a confidence level
   next to it. And here, in one sentence, is why it scored what it did."
4. **Breakdown.** > "If I tap into it, I get the four stages we just talked about: Make, Move, Pack, Last,
   each with the real fact behind it, the origin, the packaging, the lifespan. Fully explainable."
5. **Greener alternative.** > "And here is the part shoppers will love. A greener alternative in the same
   category, shown side by side, with the footprints compared, and the saving: cheaper, and lower carbon."
6. **Delivery.** > "When I add it to the basket, it asks how I want it delivered, and it gently tags the
   greenest option."
7. **The greenwashing beat.** > "Now let me go back to that very first product, the eco certified speaker.
   Watch. It carries a Cradle to Cradle label, you can see it right here in the details. But there is no
   Goede Keuze badge, and the breakdown tells you exactly why: plastic packaging, shipped from Japan, a high
   footprint. It claims green. The data does not back it. So we do not vouch for it."
8. **Basket.** > "And finally the basket. Here is that line we built up to: you saved this much money, and
   this much CO2, the equivalent of a drive to Berlin. That is the moment we want a shopper to feel."

*If the site stalls at any point, switch to the recorded video without apology. B can take a question while A recovers.*

---

## Slide 12 — Could Bol ship it (B) ~45s
> "So is this realistic for Bol. We think it is, because it builds on what Bol already runs, it does not
> replace it. Bol already labels a million products Goede Keuze, but that label is just a yes or no. We turn
> it into a ranked, explainable score, and we reuse Bol's own rule for it: a recognised certificate, or at
> least fifty percent recycled material. We also lean on Bol's real numbers. Their fit to size packing machines
> save twenty eight percent of packaging CO2 per parcel, which is why the warehouse matters in our Pack score.
> And Bol's own data shows transport is only one and a half percent of their emissions, which is exactly why
> we put most of the weight on manufacturing. Our model matches their reality."

*Cue: "matches their reality" is your feasibility mic drop. The 1.5% number shows you read their report.*

---

## Slide 13 — What's next (B) ~35s
> "If we had another month, two directions. First, circularity. Bol already refurbishes, repairs and resells,
> so we would feed that back into the score: is a refurbished version available, can this be repaired through
> Bol. The greenest product is the one nobody had to make twice. Second, scale. We would rank products by total
> impact, units sold times footprint, so Bol can see where a nudge actually saves tonnes, not grams. Our
> catalog alone already adds up to roughly two point nine million tonnes of embodied CO2. That is where to aim."

*Handoff to A for the close.*

---

## Slide 14 — Close (A) ~25s
> "So to bring it back to where we started. A winkel van ons allemaal, a shop for all of us, should also be a
> shop that is honest with all of us. One glance, the real story, and the greener choice that happens to cost
> less. Thank you."

*Cue: slow, sincere, then move to the thank you slide.*

---

## Slide 15 — Thank you and Q&A (both) ~5-7 min
> A: "We are Ctrl+V, the demo is live at this link, and we would love your questions."

Both stay at the front. Quick reference for who takes what:

| If they ask... | Lead | One line |
|---|---|---|
| Is CO2 double counted across pillars | B | No, we tested it, distance correlation is 0.12, it is a manufacturing figure |
| How did you choose the weights | B | EU Product Environmental Footprint, climate is the largest category; and they are tunable |
| Half the data is missing, why trust it | B | We do not pretend, the confidence level shows it, and it doubles as EU passport readiness |
| Are you punishing third party sellers | B | No, reward only, no storefront shaming, the badge just is not earned |
| Is the score subjective | B | The pillar mappings are our judgment, but they are transparent and defensible; the data tests are objective |
| Why does greener not cost more here | B | In this catalog price and sustainability are uncorrelated, 0.03; 81% have a cheaper greener twin |
| How is this different from Bol's Goede Keuze | A | Theirs is yes or no, ours is a ranked, explainable score that also catches the gamers |
| Would shoppers actually use it | A | It is one glance, no guilt, and the better choice is usually also cheaper |
| Hardest thing in your team | A or B | Have one honest, specific real answer ready, recruiters love this one |

*If a question stumps you, it is fine to say "we did not get to that, here is how we would approach it" and reason out loud. They are watching how you think.*
